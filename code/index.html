The Framework, In Full

The King Maker Code

Five pillars. One standard. This isn't a list of values to admire — it's a standard to hold, pillar by pillar, until holding it is just who you are.

Most frameworks are built backward — a marketing team picks five words that sound good together, and the content gets reverse-engineered to fit. The King Maker Code didn't start as words. It started as a season.

Each pillar below came out of a place where the old version of the man had to die for the next one to show up. They're listed in an order on purpose — not because life moves in a straight line, but because each one builds the ground the next one needs to stand on.

Pillar I Know Who You Are

Identity

Identity is the pillar everything else stands on, and it has nothing to do with what you do for a living or what title sits on your business card. Identity is the answer to a question most men never sit still long enough to ask: who are you before the world tries to define you?

Most men inherit an identity instead of building one — a role handed down by a father, a culture, a job, a relationship status. It works fine until it doesn't, until the role gets stripped away and there's nothing underneath it. This pillar is about doing the slower, harder work of knowing yourself well enough that no title, no job loss, no failed relationship can take it from you.

Identity Looks Like
  • Knowing who you are in the room and who you are alone — and them being the same person.
  • Defining yourself by your standard, not your circumstances.
  • Being able to lose a job, a title, or a relationship without losing yourself.
It Does Not Look Like
  • Letting your job title answer the question of who you are.
  • Needing other people's approval to know your own worth.
  • A sense of self that collapses the moment circumstances change.
From the Standard

"For years I was 'the guy who builds the business.' When the business changed, I didn't know who I was anymore. That gap is where this pillar was born."

Sit With This

If you lost the thing you lead with when you introduce yourself — what would still be true about you?

Pillar II No Exceptions

Integrity

Integrity gets thrown around as a synonym for "honesty," but that's only half of it. Integrity is structural — it's whether your stated values and your actual actions are built from the same material, with no daylight between them. A man can be honest in conversation and still be out of integrity, if what he says he values and what he actually does don't match.

Most men don't lose integrity in one dramatic collapse. They lose it in inches — the standard they hold for everyone except themselves, the value they'll defend in a conversation but quietly compromise on Tuesday. This pillar is about closing that gap, permanently, until your word and your action stop needing to be reconciled because they were never separate.

Integrity Looks Like
  • Doing the thing you said you'd do, on the day you said you'd do it.
  • Holding the same standard whether or not anyone's watching.
  • Telling people the truth before they have to ask for it.
It Does Not Look Like
  • A standard that bends depending on who's in the room.
  • Stating values you don't actually act on.
  • Explaining why this time was different, again.
From the Standard

"The first time I told a client the project was behind schedule before they noticed, something shifted. I'd rather be the man who says the hard thing on time than the one who gets caught."

Sit With This

Where's the gap right now between what you said and what you've actually done — and what would it cost you to close it this week?

Pillar III By Design, Not by Default

Intention

Intention is the difference between a life that happens to you and a life you actually build. Most men aren't failing because they're lazy — they're drifting because no one taught them that a life requires the same deliberate design as anything else worth building. Without intention, the days fill themselves in, one default decision at a time, and a year passes that nobody actually chose.

This pillar isn't about rigid five-year plans or hustle-culture scheduling. It's about putting purpose before action, every time, so that what you do each day is something you decided rather than something that simply occurred. A man living by design can tell you why he's doing what he's doing. A man living by default can only tell you how he got here.

Intention Looks Like
  • Knowing why you're doing something before you start doing it.
  • Choosing direction over comfort when the two conflict.
  • Saying no to things that don't serve the life you're actually building.
It Does Not Look Like
  • Letting your calendar fill up with other people's priorities.
  • Drifting from one default decision to the next.
  • Waking up a year later and not knowing how you got there.
From the Standard

"I spent years saying yes to whatever showed up — meetings, obligations, other people's emergencies. None of it was a decision. It was just the absence of one."

Sit With This

What's one thing on your calendar this week that you never actually chose — it just showed up and you let it stay?

Pillar IV What Actually Matters

Investment

Investment is the pillar that separates men who talk about priorities from men who actually have them. You can tell what a man values by looking at where his time, his money, and his energy actually go — not by what he says matters to him. Those three resources are finite, and where they flow is the truest statement of priority any man makes, whether he intends it or not.

This pillar isn't about working harder or earning more. It's about closing the gap between your stated priorities and your actual allocation — so the man who says family matters most is the same man whose calendar, bank account, and attention reflect that. Misalignment here doesn't announce itself. It just quietly accumulates until the gap is too wide to ignore.

Investment Looks Like
  • Your calendar and your bank statement matching what you say matters.
  • Spending energy on what compounds, not just what's urgent.
  • Treating your own growth as a real line item, not an afterthought.
It Does Not Look Like
  • Saying family matters while your calendar says otherwise.
  • Spending every dollar and every hour on what's loudest, not what's important.
  • Investing in everything except yourself.
From the Standard

"I looked at my calendar one week and realized I'd given more hours to other people's businesses than to my own family. The numbers don't lie, even when you do."

Sit With This

If someone audited your last 30 days of time and money with no context — what would they conclude actually matters to you?

Pillar V Leave It Better

Impact

Impact is the pillar that closes the loop on the other four — because identity, integrity, intention, and investment can all be held privately, for yourself alone, and still leave the world around you completely unchanged. Impact asks a different question: who is better off because you were in the room?

This isn't about legacy in the grand sense — a building with your name on it, a company that outlives you. It's smaller and more immediate than that: the people and places you touch today, left even slightly better than you found them. A son who inherits a standard instead of an absence. A team that trusts you because you've earned it, one decision at a time. Impact is the proof that the other four pillars were ever real.

Impact Looks Like
  • People around you being better off, specifically because of you.
  • Leaving every room, project, and relationship a little better than you found it.
  • Building something that outlasts your attention on it.
It Does Not Look Like
  • Keeping your growth entirely private, with no ripple effect on anyone else.
  • Taking more from rooms and relationships than you give back.
  • Confusing being impressive with actually being useful to someone.
From the Standard

"When my son was born, I realized the first four pillars were just preparation. They didn't mean anything until they showed up in how I showed up for him."

Sit With This

Name one person who is measurably better off this year because of how you showed up for them — if you can't, where does that leave you?
